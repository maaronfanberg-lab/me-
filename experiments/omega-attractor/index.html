Equations and numerical contract

For i ∈ {A,B,C}: ẋᵢ = σ(yᵢ − xᵢ) − κₓ(Lx)ᵢ ẏᵢ = xᵢ(ρᵢ − zᵢ) − yᵢ − κᵧ(Ly)ᵢ żᵢ = xᵢyᵢ − βzᵢ − κᶻ(Lz)ᵢ ρᵢ = ρ + δ(i−1) + η [exp(−τgL)]ᵢᵢ L = diag(W·1) − W; Wᵢⱼ = Wⱼᵢ ≥ 0 Σ = (I+gL)⁻¹ W₂²[N(0,Σ),N(0,I)] = Σⱼ(√sⱼ−1)² KL[N(0,Σ) ‖ N(0,I)] = ½Σⱼ(sⱼ−1−ln sⱼ) sⱼ = (1+gλⱼ)⁻¹ Ω score: let v=(xA,xB,xC)/30, T=vᵀLv, R=vᵀ(I+gL)v, μ=logistic(‖v‖), B=β₀+β₁. SΩ=e^μ tr[e^(−τgL)] + βₒlog(1+R) +γₒW₂² +ζₒB +ξₒT²/(1+T²). Method: fixed-step classical RK4 for the 9D state and its 9D tangent vector. Tangent-vector normalization gives a finite-time exponent estimate; it is not a proof of chaos.
What counts as mathematically accurate?

The differential equations, graph Laplacian, matrix spectrum, spectral heat, covariance metric and Gaussian distances are precisely defined. The displayed lines are numerical approximations to trajectories. A finite simulation cannot prove an attractor exists, certify its fractal dimension, or establish a positive asymptotic Lyapunov exponent. Some parameter settings approach equilibria instead of chaotic sets. Editing the time step changes numerical accuracy, not the underlying ODE.

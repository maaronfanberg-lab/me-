# Corrections

A ledger of claims made while building this project: what was claimed, the test that could prove it wrong,
and what happened. Entries are never deleted. A claim that turned out wrong stays here with its correction.

Status key: **held** (survived its test so far), **partly wrong**, **wrong**, **untested**.

| # | Claim | Test | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Training a deep network drives its depth geometry toward flat (κ → 0). | 136 runs, 24-layer tanh MLPs on digits, four starting geometries, controls with random-direction updates. | Slope settled near zero from both the vanishing and the exploding side; random-direction updates did not flatten. Later contradicted at 6 layers (entry 4). | **partly wrong** |
| 2 | Adam makes the depth geometry steeper. | Same 136 runs, more seeds. | The steepening came from runs that failed to train. Where Adam trained well its slope also ended near zero; what Adam reliably did was roughen the profile layer to layer. | **wrong** |
| 3 | Networks that end flat are the ones that learned. | 62 successful vs 10 failed SGD runs. | Every success ended near flat, but so did 8 of the 10 failures. Flatness looked necessary, not sufficient. | **partly wrong** |
| 4 | Training holds the *total* tilt (output to first layer) roughly fixed, whatever the depth. | Experiment 1: 144 runs, depths 3–96, plain and residual, tanh and ReLU, two tasks. | Held for tanh networks once a measurement artifact was removed (entry 8): from 12 to 96 layers the corrected tilt stays within about 0.1–0.4 of a constant. The constant depends on the task (≈ +1.3 on digits, ≈ +2.2 on circle), so the earlier "3–6× louder" was one task's value, not a universal number. Deep residual ReLU networks drift (spread up to 1.0), and plain networks past 24–48 layers never trained at all. | **held, with limits** |
| 5 | The lapse is each layer's learning clock: weights change in proportion to N. | Experiment 2: per-layer weight motion vs lapse over the first 200 steps, SGD vs Adam, three networks. | Under SGD weight motion follows lapse × the layer's input activity, not the lapse alone. In residual networks activity is nearly uniform and the lapse alone predicts motion (log-log slope 0.94, r = 0.95). In a plain σ_w = 0.8 network the lapse varies ×300 across layers but forward activity varies ×400 the other way, so every layer learns at nearly the same speed. Under Adam the link is gone (slope ≈ 0.05), as predicted. | **partly wrong** |
| 6 | "120 runs" were launched. | Counting the job list. | There were 136. A count was reported without being checked. | **wrong** |
| 7 | Sigmoid starting weights of scale 3 cancel sigmoid's ¼ gain and remove the input horizon. | 6-layer circle network in the Lapse Playground, 4 seeds. | First layer heard ×0.89 of the output with scale 3, versus ×0.0000008 with scale 1. | **held** |
| 8 | (Measurement) The raw tilt of residual networks falls steadily with depth. | Checked against the architecture: each residual block's error signal carries the fixed factor α = 1/√depth that the output layer does not. | The fall was the α factor, not learning. Removing it (T* = T − ln α, now `geometry_deltas(remove_alpha=True)`, with a test that the shift is exactly −ln α) flattens the residual tanh curves to within ±0.1 from 12 to 96 layers. Both raw and corrected numbers are reported. The correction was found after seeing the data, which is stated wherever it is used. | **wrong (artifact), fixed** |
| 9 | (Analysis) Regressing weight motion on lapse × activity tests the clock law. | Compared the ranges across layers directly. | When lapse × activity is nearly constant across layers (the σ_w = 0.8 network), the regression slope is meaningless noise (seeds gave −2.38, 0.55, −0.12). The ranges, not the slope, are the right test. | **wrong (method), fixed** |
| 10 | A plain σ_w = 1.0 network's weight motion should spread as much as lapse × activity predicts. | Experiment 5: lapse, activity, alignment and motion all measured on the same 200 steps and batches. | The ×15–19 prediction was an analysis error: it multiplied lapse averaged over 200 steps by activity at step 0, and these networks reshape within those steps. On one time base, ‖δ‖·‖h‖/‖W‖ predicts ×4.1–4.4 against a measured ×2.9–3.2, and the per-layer alignment (×1.2–1.3) accounts for most of the remainder. | **resolved (my analysis error)** |
| 11 | (README) In the plain σ_w = 0.8 network, activity spreads ×360–510 against the lapse; plain σ_w = 1.0 activity spreads ×8. | Re-measured on one time base (experiment 5, part 3). | The same mixed-window error as entry 10. Activity actually spreads ×105–139 (σ_w = 0.8) and ×3.2–3.3 (σ_w = 1.0). The qualitative claim, that opposite-running activity largely cancels a 300-fold lapse difference, still holds: the product spreads ×4–7, and alignment covers most of the rest. README corrected. | **wrong numbers, claim held** |
| 12 | (Pre-registered, experiment 4) The trained total tilt is set by one task property: input dimension, input scale, class count, or label noise. | 60 runs changing one property at a time, pass rule fixed in advance. | Input dimension and input scale do not move it. Class count moves it for residual networks (+1.06 for 10 → 2 classes, matching the predicted ≈ +0.9) but not plain ones. Label noise moves it in opposite directions on the two tasks, contradicting the stated prediction. Two input-dimension tests could not be scored because those networks never learned. No single property explains it. | **wrong as a single-cause claim; class count held for residual nets** |
| 13 | (Analysis) An exploratory split of the tilt, first attempt. | Checked that internal part + output step add back to the total. | They did not: the stored profiles already had α removed and I multiplied by √24 again. Fixed, and the table now carries a per-row sum check (largest error 4e−16). | **wrong, fixed** |
| 14 | (Pre-registered, experiment 6) The trained tilt is set by how well the network fits its training data (H1), and/or by class count (H2). | Tilt read at matched training loss across 8 conditions; within-run correlation of tilt with log loss. | Within runs the tilt rose as loss fell in 18 of 24 runs, short of the pre-set 20 (all fit-able runs track at −0.99 to −1.00; the exceptions are the unfittable noisy circle tasks). | 15 | (Qualifies entry 4) Experiment 1's depth-independent tilt values describe a trained network. | Experiment 6 shows the tilt keeps rising as loss falls. | Those values were read at a fixed 4,000 steps, not at a fixed loss, so they are snapshots of a running quantity. | 17 | (Pre-registered, experiment 7) For residual tanh networks, the tilt does not depend on depth (12–96) when depths are compared at the same training loss. | 60 runs, 5 depths, 2 activations, 2 tasks; pass rule fixed in advance. | Held on both tasks: no pair of depths crossed twice the combined seed spread at any loss level. | 19 | (Exploratory, experiment 8) The deep-ReLU tilt problem is a property of the whole depth of the network. | T* split exactly into output, stream-transport and first-block gate parts (`lapse/probe.py`, checked to sum to rounding). | Partly. | 20 | (Qualifies entries 4 and 17) The near-constant tanh tilt across depth means nothing about the network changes with depth. | Same split, tanh, depths 12–96, at loss 0.01. | Transport rises with depth (digits +0.75 → +0.91, circle +1.52 → +1.81), while the output part falls slightly (+0.53 → +0.49, +0.93 → +0.82).

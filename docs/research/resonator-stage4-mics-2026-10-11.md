# RESONATOR Stage 4 — more mics (2026-10-11)

Preview only (`apps/resonator-next.html`); generated by `qa-resonator/patch_s4.py`.

## 1. Observed problem
Anukari can listen to a structure through several microphones and route each one separately:
"send each mic to its own bus in your DAW", up to 50 outputs (anukari.com/features, read 2026-10-11).
Mics "can both be mapped to either mono channels or stereo pairs" (release notes 0.9.10, 2025-07-04),
and "presets with lots of mics now perform much better" (0.9.23, 2025-12-13). RESONATOR had one main
pickup, plus a second listening point used only by the Width control.

**Research question.** Can the phone build hear a body from up to four places at once, with each extra
mic's level and stereo position set separately? Nothing may change while the extra mics are off.

## 2–5. Evidence and mechanism
- **What a mic hears.** Each extra mic works the same way as the main mic's point pickup. It takes the
  velocity of one mass along the mic direction, blended with displacement by the Blend knob. That
  signal then goes through the same two-pole smoothing at every substep, the same DC blocker and the
  same soft clip. An extra mic at 100% on the main mic's own ball therefore adds about the main mic's
  level (measured below).
- **Polyphony.** Each mic hears every voice. Voices are summed and scaled by 1/√voices, as the main
  path does.
- **Pan.** Panning uses constant-power amplitude panning: g_L² + g_R² is constant, so a mic keeps its
  loudness as it moves across (Pulkki, "Virtual sound source positioning using vector base amplitude
  panning", JAES 45(6), 456–466, 1997). That paper places this within the stereophonic sine and
  tangent laws going back to Blumlein. Here the gains are cos and sin of (pan + 1)·π/4, scaled so a
  centred mic is at unity in each channel. Hard left leaves the right channel bit-identical (proof 1).
- **Clean mics.** Mics 2–4 skip the Amp tab's chain (coil pickups, amp, cabinet). They are room mics.
  Anukari gives every mic its own compressor; here everything passes through the existing true-peak
  limiter instead (the worst case is measured below).
- **Pinned balls.** A pinned ball never moves, so the engine refuses a mic on it. The page applies the
  same rule after every rebuild and re-sends Mics 2–4, so the screen and the sound always agree. This
  covers pin, link, add, delete, undo and presets. Undo and redo carry the mic positions with the body.
  Delete renumbers them, and a new shape starts with them off.
- **Cost.** At each substep, every (mic, voice) pair is one entry in a short list. The list is built
  once per audio block, because pitch, scale and pan only change between blocks. On 7 test setups the
  output is identical to the straightforward version.

## 6–8. Competing explanations, limits, context
- **Tried and withdrawn: a coil that followed the springs.** The Amp tab's coil pickups average
  neighbouring masses chosen by mass number. A real pickup senses a stretch of string: Paiva, Pakarinen
  & Välimäki ("Acoustics and modeling of pickups", JAES 60(10), 768–782, 2012) model this "sensitivity
  width" as a low-pass effect, and point-pickup models are known to ignore it (Mohamad, Harte & Dixon,
  DAFx-17, 2017). On hand-built bodies, mass numbers stop matching physical neighbours. So a first
  version counted neighbours along springs, switched on once a body had been edited.
  - **Measured, it was wrong to ship.** Switching it on changed the main pickup's sound by itself,
    with no physical change at all. In pickup modes 1–2 the change reached +13.9 dB (Twins) and
    brightness shifts of +59% (Tine) and −56% (Web).
  - **Why.** On 2-D and 3-D shapes the window changed shape. On strings, the second coil moved to the
    other side of the main mic.
  - **Outcome.** Withdrawn. The coil is the live page's coil (live vs preview bit-identical, including
    pickup modes 1–2). The other side, that a pickup wide enough to span a cut also hears the far
    piece, is physically legitimate for a spatial aperture.
  - **Possible later.** A coil that is continuous across edits (positions along the body carried
    through edits). It is not needed for Mics 2–4.
- **Ratchet.** Two errors were missed until this stage's last pass, and each now has a permanent check:
  - **A body-level flag changed the sound by itself.** The new check: edits that change nothing
    physical (delete then undo, pin then undo, pin then unpin) must send the engine exactly the
    untouched body. A mutation test confirms the check fails when the old flag is put back.
  - **Page and engine disagreed after pinning a mic's ball, and after undo.** Both cases now have
    checks.
- **Limits.**
  - All mics share the main mic's direction.
  - There is no per-mic compressor and no per-mic output bus. A browser page has one stereo output.
  - In poly mode, the main mic's stereo second point still hears only voice 0 (pre-existing).
- **Phone.** CPU was measured only on the dev machine; phone numbers need the in-app Benchmark. Also
  seen while measuring (pre-existing, not Stage 4): 8 voices on String run at about 110% of real time
  on the dev machine. This goes to the phone-performance stage.

## 9. Implementation mapping
- **Engine.**
  - New message `xmic {k, i, g, p}`.
  - `build()` drops mics on missing or pinned masses and clears their filters.
  - `vectors()` gives each mic its direction.
  - A per-block tap list feeds the per-substep filters.
  - Each mic gets a DC blocker, soft clip, level and constant-power pan into the stereo mix, before the
    effects and the limiter.
- **Page (Mic tab → More mics).**
  - Mic 1–4 chips.
  - Extra mic level (0–150%, default 70%) and Extra mic pan, with tap steps.
  - Remove this mic.
  - The mic tool places whichever mic is picked.
  - Markers labelled "mic 2–4".

# RESONATOR Stage 1 — tuning off the audio thread, octave-trap fix, phone diagnostics (2026-10-11)

Preview page: `apps/resonator-next.html` (launcher card "Resonator next"). The live
`apps/resonator.html` is unchanged until Alex approves the preview on his phone.
Generated by `qa-resonator/patch_s1.py` (verified edits; aborts on any mismatch).

## 1. Observed problem
- Choosing Tank froze the audio clock for **911 ms**; Stiffness + on Tank then a note froze it
  for **534 ms** (Chromium, measured with `AudioContext.currentTime`; a phone is slower).
- Cause (profiled 2026-10-10): `calibrate()` (eigen-solves of the stiffness matrix) runs inside the
  AudioWorklet, i.e. on the audio rendering thread.
- The octave trap (see `resonator-output-safety-2026-10-09.md`): the tuned partial depended on
  the order of strike/mic moves and Stiffness/Substrate changes.

## 2–3. Foundational and current evidence
- The AudioWorklet processor's `process()` runs on the audio rendering thread and is called
  isochronously per render quantum (Chrome team, "Audio Worklet is now available by default",
  developer.chrome.com/blog/audio-worklet). A 128-frame quantum at 48 kHz is 2.67 ms, so a 355 ms
  calculation misses ~130 consecutive deadlines.
- Messages through one MessagePort are one task source and are processed in order; ordering
  **across** ports is not guaranteed (WHATWG list, Wilson/Hickson, 2009-08,
  lists.whatwg.org/pipermail/whatwg-whatwg.org/2009-August/064059.html).

## 5. Mechanism
Move the calculation, not the symptom: a shadow copy of the same engine code runs in a Web Worker,
receives every control message the audio engine receives (never notes or buffer returns), and
computes the table there. The page alone sequences messages (because cross-channel order is not
guaranteed): a new body is held back and sent to the audio engine together with its table; notes
that need a fresh table wait for it. The audio engine keeps rendering the old body meanwhile.

Octave trap: modes are always ranked with the strike/mic vectors captured when the body is built,
so later strike/mic moves cannot change which partial is tuned.

## 6–7. Competing explanations, limits
- Could the stall be the solver alone? Partly: F5 (2026-10-10) cut Tank's tuning 9×, but 355 ms
  (≈1 s in the browser) still blocks; only moving it off the thread removes the freeze.
- Notes pressed while a body is being tuned now start when the table is ready (they did before
  too, behind a frozen engine). Ringing notes are no longer interrupted.
- Debounced retuning (30 ms) means a note within a retune window waits up to the tuning time.
- If Workers are unavailable or the shadow fails, the engine tunes itself as before.

## 8. Context transfer
Web Workers and Blob URLs are standard in iPhone Safari; the fallback covers the rest. Phone
timing is not inferred from this machine: the Copy report button reports the measured tuning
times from the phone itself.

## 9. Implementation mapping
- Engine: `calibrate()` → reference-pickup wrapper around `calibrateNow()`; `calState()/applyCal()`;
  messages `cal`, `asyncCal`; `build()` accepts a supplied table; note-time retune skipped when async.
- Page: `TUNER` (Worker from Blob, `node.port.postMessage` routed through `tunerRoute`),
  20 s answer timeout, 4 s start-up timeout, failure fallback; "tuning…" in the readout;
  `Copy report` button.

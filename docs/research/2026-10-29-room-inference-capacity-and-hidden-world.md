## Foundational, current, and natural-behavior evidence (gates 2-4)
- Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson (1974), *Language* 50, 696-735, doi:10.2307/412243: locally managed turn-taking is recipient-sensitive; no guarantee that turn selection alone causes topic progress.
- Clark & Wilkes-Gibbs (1986), *Cognition* 22, 1-39, doi:10.1016/0010-0277(86)90010-7: mutual reference is progressively established through expansion, repair and acceptance; agents should transfer and verify distinct facts, not mirror phrases.
- Loewenstein (1994), *Psychological Bulletin*, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75: knowledge gaps can motivate curiosity. Transfer to LLM characters is a hypothesis, not a proven mechanism.
- Zhou et al. (2024), *EMNLP*, https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1208/: non-omniscient LLM social simulations can be *harder*, not easier, than omniscient simulations; this cautions against treating private information as a guaranteed fix.
- Wang et al. (2024), *ACL*, https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331/: multi-agent discussion is not automatically superior to a single strong agent; compare baselines, not just plausibility.
- Current llama.cpp server docs, https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md : `-np` controls inference slots and continuous batching shares decoder resources. The server has a single main decoding context as described in README-dev.md. This supports a capacity-controlled trial, not a universal claim that serialization is faster.

## Mechanism and counterarguments (gates 5-7)
**Capacity intervention**: run one private model request at a time in the existing beat, configure one slot, and bound output tokens to 192 comprehension, 256 thought, 320 structured expression. Keep all other response validation and publication gates unchanged. It should reduce request queue timeouts but may increase beat wall-clock time and truncate otherwise valid structured output. Token caps are an engineering hypothesis to be tested.

**Conversation intervention (separate experiment)**: participants each have a different *fictional, explicitly sourced* local observation of one shared situation. A speaker can reveal their own observation, ask the others about theirs, or challenge apparent contradictions. Do not forge their shared past, invent other participants' knowledge, or reveal private cards in a shared prompt. Preserve organic off-topic transitions, disagreements, and silence. A successful test must demonstrate questions that lead to information transfer, clarification, and actual change in common understanding. It may instead produce stilted interrogations or repeated puzzle-solving, so evaluate that possibility explicitly.

## Context transfer and implementation (gates 8-9)
Human dyads, controlled tasks and small LLMs are not the same as four longitudinal, voluntary characters. Test separate epistemic contexts without scripting responses or mandating topic words. For capacity, change the real workflow `.github/workflows/sarah-society.yml`, model request bound `scripts/room_private_model/__init__.py`, and a regression guard. For hidden-world, introduce a *disabled-by-default* experimental provider and an isolated evaluation, without shipping untested story cards to the public conversation.

## Second mechanism candidate: infrequent environmental event (staged; not yet activated)
In early trial turns, providing an optional private fact in the system context may be insufficient to break a strong existing conversational attractor. A distinct mechanism is to make a real *fictional-world change* occur AFTER the previous public utterance, with a private local observation and a common event. This changes the conversational situation rather than requesting a new topic or forbidding old words. It is scheduled only at most two onsets during the trial, at pre-state cycles 35175 and 35220; observed output is one published cycle later because the engine reads the last committed cycle while generating the next. The original experiment remains otherwise unchanged. We will retain separate measurements before and after the first event rather than attributing both to the same condition. Contradictory explanation: any ensuing shift might arise from the explicit event wording or ordinary random drift, not information asymmetry itself. Prematurely introducing this variant would confound the original canary; merge only after confirming that the initial optional-cue period remained inert.

### Competing explanations and limitations
The earlier 35175 event was the *same radio scene already active*, not a genuinely different subject. That explains its inability to change the stalled trajectory even without assuming that Llama 3B is incapable of social response. A more persistent cue could also become a repeated prompt-shaped anchor, destroying autonomous topic changes. A scene clue introduced as a forged public participant message would corrupt real turn provenance; the fix intentionally retains a separate external-environment channel instead. No topic-word bans, synthetic model speech, or mandatory speaker quotas are introduced.

### Implementation and falsification
A genuinely new community-garden situation begins at pre-state **35200**, with lantern festival at **35220**. Each speaker receives only that speaker's private observation, plus shared environmental situation, after the actual latest three public turns in a separate prompt section for no more than **12 consecutive cycles after onset**. An addressed visitor's prompt overrides all experiment material. The natural selected-floor and publication validators remain authoritative. After the 12-cycle window, all extra situational cues expire even if the group never used them; that prevents indefinite topic lock. The base private fact continues for the larger finite experiment but is optional and does not pretend to be an autobiographical memory. Compare 35161–35199 private-only baseline to 35201–35212 persistent world grounder and 35221–35232 second environment, while recognizing historical carry-over and phase changes. Success requires at least six of ten consecutive turns showing concrete specific details beyond parroting scene nouns and at least three responsive uptake/repair/fact-transfer pairs across two characters; failure includes one-sentence novelty followed by abstract metaphors, agent knowledge leaks, or word-for-word repetitions. Human adjudication is necessary beyond lexical proxies.

## 2. Question

The data shows that on the main cardioid (depth 1) the law's miss grows with q instead of shrinking. It goes from 0 at 1/2, to −0.019 at 1/3, to about +0.10 at q = 17. It also jumps around between fractions with the same q. Why does a first-order law get worse on smaller buds, and what does the miss depend on?

Background: the law r = |dc/dλ at root| / q² is the linear term of the child's multiplier map at its root. The measured radius is |dc/dμ| at the child's centre, μ = 0. So the surprise ln(measured/predicted) measures how far the child's map c(μ) bends between μ = 1 and μ = 0, which is the bud's shape. Guckenheimer and McGehee explain the 1/N² scaling with normal forms of resonant bifurcations. They point to Shishikura's work on bifurcation of parabolic fixed points for the *limiting shapes*. Fowler and McGuinness (2019) give an analytic estimate and call the bulbs "approximate circles". Their formula was not readable from the abstract page, so it was not compared here.

## 3. Hypotheses and refutation tests

An exploratory scan (`1/q`, `2/q`, Farey approaches to 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 2/5, up to q ≈ 320) came first and suggested the hypotheses below. They were written down before the confirming runs.

- **H1.** Hold the numerator p fixed and let q grow (buds approaching the cusp at c = 1/4). The surprise converges to a constant that depends on p alone. *Refuted if* the differences between successive values do not shrink as q doubles, or if two sequences with the same p settle at different values.
- **H1′** (added after H1 failed, then tested on fresh q values). The limit depends on the *phase* r/p, where q = pk + r. That is where the bud sits between the 1/k and 1/(k+1) buds. This is the coordinate that parabolic-implosion theory predicts. *Refuted if* two q values with the same p and the same q mod p disagree by more than numerical error, at q values not used to form the hypothesis.
- **H2.** Along a Farey approach to a rational a/b that is not the cusp (k/(2k+1) → 1/2, k/(3k+1) → 1/3), the surprise converges like 1/q. *Refuted if* the Richardson extrapolations 2·S(2q) − S(q) from successive pairs disagree by more than 0.002.
- **Test-suspicion check.** Recompute the radius at 60 digits with the independent decimal Newton solver from `verify.py`, and recompute the prediction exactly. On the main cardioid |dc/dλ| = sin(πp/q), so predicted = sin(πp/q)/q². Any surprise pattern smaller than the disagreement between the two methods is not real.

## 4. Method

All scripts call `engine.grow` from the seed, or the decimal functions copied out of `verify.py`. The stored data is never modified. For q beyond the engine's precision floor (radius < 1e-8), the centre is found by 60-digit Newton, starting from the law's own guess. The radius then comes from a 60-digit difference of the multiplier, and the prediction from the exact sin formula. Total run time was a few minutes on the visit machine, so nothing was sent to Actions.

## 6. Result

- **H1 refuted.** For p = 3 the two residue classes settle at 0.115777 and 0.115271. They differ by 5e-4, which is about 10⁷ times the numerical error.
- **H1′ survived** on q values not used to form it. Same p and same q mod p give the same surprise to 6 digits (3/481 = 3/640, 3/320 = 3/962). The limits so far are: phase 0 (the 1/q buds) ≈ 0.03467, still creeping up by less than 1e-5 per doubling of q; phase 1/2 ≈ 0.10338; phase 1/4 ≈ 0.11278; phase 1/3 ≈ 0.115777; phase 2/3 ≈ 0.115271. Phases 1/3 and 2/3 are *not* symmetric.
- **H2 survived.** The extrapolated limits settle within 1e-4 (1/2 → 0.0264, 1/3 from below → 0.0218). Near an ordinary parabolic root the approach is slow, about 1/q. Near the cusp, fixed-p sequences settle much faster, except for a small slow drift in the 1/q family.
- **What this explains.** The law's miss on the main cardioid is not error that should vanish as buds get smaller. It is a shape constant. Near a parabolic point, the rescaled buds approach limiting shapes, and the miss approaches the value for the limiting shape at that bud's position. That position is set by the phase, not by q. This is a numerical pattern consistent with the known limiting-shape picture (Shishikura; Guckenheimer–McGehee). It is not a proof, and the constants above are measured, not derived.
- **Limits.** Main cardioid only. Deeper buds (depth ≥ 2) have parents whose multiplier maps are not exact quadratics, and they were not tested. The two-sided 1/3 comparison used non-mirror sequences, so it says nothing about symmetry. The slow drift of the 1/q family is not yet explained.

## 7. What changed and what did not

- **Nothing in the plant changed.** No expectation model was added. The finding is solid for depth 1, but depth-1 buds are 7% of the plant (97 of 1344). The class key (q, min(p, q−p), depth) already predicts each mirror bud from its twin. A prior for first-of-class depth-1 buds would change only the first residual of a few classes. That is too small a payoff to justify a new expectation version before the deeper buds are understood. Also, byte-identical replay cannot be confirmed on this machine (see Health), which is another reason not to touch the engine from here.
- Data files, `engine.py`, `grow.py` and `verify.py` were not touched. Only this note was added.

## 9. Next question

Do buds deeper in the tree carry the same phase-dependent constant? For example, take the p/q children of the period-2 disc, which is an exact circle. If their surprises match the main-cardioid limits at the same phase, a single "shape prior" keyed on phase could become expectation model v2. If they don't, the parent's own distortion is part of the miss. A related puzzle from the data: 1/2 buds average +0.043 at depth 2 but −0.048 at depth 3.

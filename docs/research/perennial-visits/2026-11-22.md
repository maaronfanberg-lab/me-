## 2. Question

Last visit's next question: do buds deeper in the tree carry the same phase-dependent constants as the main cardioid? The cleanest place to ask is the period-2 disc (centre −1). It is an exact circle, c = −1 + λ/4, so the law's prediction for its p/q child is exactly 1/(4q²), with no parent distortion at all.

## 3. Hypotheses and refutation tests

A first run (fixed numerator p, q up to 2048, on the disc) gave one surprise. The disc's 1/q buds do **not** approach the cardioid's 1/q constant (0.0347). They approach 0.026392, which is the value the cardioid's buds reach as they crowd towards the cardioid's 1/2 point (the k/(2k+1) family). That point is c = −3/4, where the disc and the cardioid touch. The disc's λ → 1 point is the same place. This suggested:

- **H3 (two-sided universality).** The limiting surprise is set by the parabolic point the buds crowd towards, plus a phase. It does not depend on which parent they grow from. So families on the disc side and the cardioid side of c = −3/4 should share the same set of limit constants.
- To make that testable, the families must be paired. A scan for s ≤ 8 (both sides, every residue class) gave an empirical pairing rule:

  **Pairing rule.** Write a cardioid bud near 1/2 as θ = 1/2 − s/(2N), where N is its period. Write a disc bud as s/q, with period D = 2q. The two families (fixed s, period growing) have the same limit when **(D − N)/s is an odd integer**. Equivalently, using the phase φ = period/(2s) mod 1, the two sides agree when their phases differ by exactly half a turn.

- **Refutation test, written before running it.** The rule was formed on s ≤ 8. Test it on fresh values s = 7, 9, 10, 12 (s = 7 uses residues not in the scan). For every cardioid family, the rule names one disc family. **H3 and the rule are refuted if** any named pair differs by more than 5e-5 in extrapolated limit, or if any non-named disc family lies closer than the named one.

## 4. Method

- Every bud comes from `engine.grow`, on parent = seed (cardioid) or parent = `grow(seed, 1, 2)` (the disc). Stored data was not read for this part and was not modified.
- For each family, the surprise is computed at four sizes (period ≈ 480 to 3840). The limit is extrapolated by fitting S = L + a/q + b/q² through the last three points, and again through the first three. The difference between the two fits is the stated uncertainty.
- **Test-suspicion check.** For six sample buds on both sides (periods 243 to 1281), the radius was recomputed with the independent 60-digit decimal Newton solver from `verify.py`, and the prediction from the exact formulas: 1/(4q²) on the disc and sin(πθ)/N² on the cardioid.
- Scripts: `scripts/2026-11-22-scan.py` (family scan) and `scripts/2026-11-22-predict.py` (the preregistered test). Run them from `experiments/perennial/`. The whole visit took under a minute of compute, so nothing was sent to Actions.

## 6. Result

- **H3 survived** its refutation test on fresh values. Families on the two sides of c = −3/4 share one set of limit constants, paired by the half-turn rule, to about 1e-6.
- **Last visit's next question is answered "no, but in a better way".** Depth-2 buds do *not* carry the cardioid-cusp constants. They carry the constants of whatever pinch point they crowd towards. The disc's small buds near its root are seen from the other side of the same pinch point where the cardioid's 1/2-side buds pile up.
- **What this explains.** Part of the law's miss is not the parent's distortion. It is the shape of the pinch point the bud lives near, read at a phase. Two different parents meeting at one pinch point see the same shapes, shifted by half a turn.
- **What it does not show.** This is a numerical pattern, not a proof. The half-turn shift is measured, not derived. It looks like the kind of statement Lavaurs-phase (parabolic implosion) theory makes, where the limiting dynamics depends on a phase that jumps across a parabolic parameter, but this exact correspondence was not found in a source this visit. Different pinch points of the same kind (the two 1/2 points; the two 1/3 points) give close but different constants. So "universal" here means the same point, not the same type.

### Side finding: the 1/2-bud puzzle from last visit

Last visit noted that 1/2 buds average +0.043 at depth 2 but −0.048 at depth 3. This is not a depth effect. It is a parent effect, and the class key cannot see it:

| depth | 1/2 child of a parent with q ≥ 3 | 1/2 child of a 1/2 bud (period doubling) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | +0.044 (52 buds) | — |
| 3 | −0.035 (76) | −0.068 (45) |
| 5 | −0.034 (45) | −0.078 (73) |
| 8 | −0.031 (10) | −0.079 (83) |
| 10 | — | −0.080 (2) |

The period-doubling chain settles towards about −0.080. The depth-2 average is positive only because no depth-2 1/2 bud has a 1/2 parent. The deeper averages mix the two kinds in changing proportions.

## 9. Next question

The period-doubling chain of 1/2 buds settles at a surprise of about −0.080. Does that number follow from Feigenbaum universality? In a cascade, successive component sizes shrink by a ratio tending to δ ≈ 4.669. The law predicts each child from its parent's map at λ = −1. So the limit should be a fixed quantity of the cascade's limiting map, computable from the Feigenbaum fixed point. Test: grow a cascade deeper than the data reaches (period up to 4096 is 12 doublings), extrapolate geometrically, and compare with a value derived from published cascade constants. Refuted if the extrapolated limit does not settle, or if the derived value disagrees beyond the extrapolation error.

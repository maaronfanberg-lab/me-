# RESONATOR Stage 3 — new exciters and key release (2026-10-11)

Preview only (`apps/resonator-next.html`); generated by `qa-resonator/patch_s3.py`.

## 1. Observed problem
Anukari excites structures with mallets, bows, plectrums, oscillators ("classic waveforms with
ADSR") and audio input (anukari.com/features); turbulence arrived after 1.0 (devlog, Aug 2026).
RESONATOR had mallet, Worthington splash, bow and mic feedback, and keys had no release.

## 2–5. Evidence and mechanism
- **Plectrum.** A pluck is a displacement initial condition: the string is pulled aside and released,
  so partials fall roughly as 1/m² (displacement), darker than a strike's flat velocity spectrum
  (J. O. Smith, *Physical Audio Signal Processing*, plucked vs struck string; Cuzzucoli & Lombardo,
  Computer Music Journal 23(2), 1999; Evangelista & Eckerholm, IEEE TASLP 18(4), 2010, on the
  player's touch and release). Implemented as a smooth pull (5–30 ms, scaled by pitch) and a
  release whose length the Mallet knob sets (pick ≈ 0.1 ms → finger ≈ 4–5 ms).
- **Oscillator.** A force at the strike point at the played pitch, 12 ms attack, 150 ms release on key
  up. Measured: harmonics of the drive barely sound, because a body only rings at its own partials
  (String partials sit at 1.98×, 2.94×, not 2×, 3×). So "hard" adds a little broadband roughness, which
  reaches the upper partials; "soft" is a pure sine.
- **Wind.** Independent low-passed (400 Hz) noise force on every free mass, from the engine's seeded
  generator, so a given setting always renders the same way.
- **Mic in.** The phone microphone's signal is a force at the strike point.
- **Level normalisation.** A fixed force deflects a stiff drumhead far less than a loose hoop
  (first measurement: plectrum 0.13× to 4.9× the mallet across shapes). The new exciters therefore
  aim for a *displacement*: force = target × aChar ÷ (static compliance at the strike point), where
  compliance = Σ over elastic modes of (mode shape at the strike point)² ÷ λ, from the same modal
  solve the tuning already does (sent across from the background tuner).
- **Key release.** Keys send a note-off on release; it releases the oscillator. Strike-tool auditions
  release themselves; leaving Oscillator mode releases held notes.

## 6–8. Competing explanations, limits, context
- iPhone: while the microphone is on, Web Audio plays at about half volume; WebKit bug 236219 is still
  open and reproduces on iOS 26.4 (2026-03). Switching `navigator.audioSession.type` back to
  "playback" when the mic goes off restores the volume (workaround from that bug). The page says so
  next to the button and recommends headphones (no feedback). Echo cancellation is on by default in
  Safari since 2023 (WebKit 264721@main).
- The remaining level spread across shapes (≈6×) is real character: a free Chime bar takes a pluck
  very differently from a pinned drumhead. The loudest case reaches the limiter ceiling safely.
- Found while reading this code: the "rigid modes are lowest" safety check in the fast solver (F5)
  was never armed (`elastic` never incremented). Tested: it never changed a tuning — Substrate adds
  the same stiffness to every mode, so the three drift modes are always lowest — fixed anyway. The
  solver port also omits JAMA's final sort; every caller already re-sorts.
- Found while testing: each shape change re-sends every knob, which made the background tuner tune
  twice. It now waits for that burst (12 ms, at most ~70 ms) and tunes once; the result is identical.

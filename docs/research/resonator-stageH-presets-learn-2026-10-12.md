# RESONATOR Stage H — factory presets and the Learn tab (2026-10-12)

Preview only (`apps/resonator-next.html`). Generated by `qa-resonator/patch_h1.py`, a verified edit
that aborts on any mismatch. Page only: the engine source is byte-identical to the one before this

## 1. Observed problem
- Alex, after the first working build: "now I just need proficiency to use it." The instrument offers
  dozens of controls and no guided way to learn what each one does to the sound.
- Anukari ships "over 200 curated factory presets" so users can "start exploring immediately"
  (rekkerd.org, 2026-09-11, read 2026-10-12). RESONATOR shipped none: every session started from the
  default string.
- Found while building this stage (errors, not taste):
  - **Presets reloaded an octave down** when saved with the strike and mic near an end. Measured: a
    15-mass string reloaded with its reference mode at 622 Hz instead of 314 Hz.
  - Loading a preset **did not tell the engine its spectrum choice**. The chip changed; the sound did
    not.
  - **Mic axis X** (0) came back as Y, because 0 was read as "missing".
  - A preset's **bow** state was not applied.
  - The **axis chips, scale chips and the shape's description** kept showing the previous instrument.
  - **"Loaded …" messages were wiped** by the readout's next redraw, usually within a moment.

**Research question.** Can a short set of guided comparisons make the controls learnable on a phone,
with every explanation true of this engine? Can presets be a reliable starting point?

**Pre-change baseline.**
- No presets shipped; no lessons.
- The restore errors above.

**Validation criteria (set before the change).**
- Every number a lesson quotes is re-measured from what the page sends, at all three engine rates, and
  the shipped text contains it.
- Each lesson's A and B change only the one thing named and play the same key at the same strength.
- The explanation stays closed until both A and B have been heard.
- The user's instrument comes back exactly: shape, knobs, bow, axis, spectrum, scale, pickups and
  tuning.
- Every factory preset plays cleanly at roughly the default instrument's loudness, and its
  description is measured true.
- A saved preset reloads with the same partial tuned.
- Earlier presets still load.
- All earlier suites pass, and every control works by tap.

## 2–3. Sources (read 2026-10-12)
- **Lesson design: guess first.** C. Crouch, A. P. Fagen, J. P. Callan and E. Mazur, "Classroom
  demonstrations: Learning tools or entertainment?", *Am. J. Phys.* 72(6), 835–838 (2004),
  doi:10.1119/1.1707018 (abstract via ComPADRE). Students who only watched a demonstration understood
  it no better than students who never saw it. Students who **predicted the outcome first** showed
  significantly greater understanding.
  - Hence each lesson asks a question, then plays A and B, and opens the explanation only after both.
- **Brightness.**
  - E. Schubert and J. Wolfe, "Does timbral brightness scale with frequency and spectral centroid?",
    *Acta Acustica united with Acustica* 92, 820–825 (2006). The plain spectral centroid "was a good
    predictor of perceived brightness."
  - Hence "brightness centre", the energy-weighted mean frequency, is the number the lessons quote.
- **Damping.** J. O. Smith, "Wave equation" notes (CCRMA, ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/asahb04). In the
  damped string equation:
  - one term damps all frequencies alike (RESONATOR's Decay, lesson 2);
  - another damping "increases with frequency" (Damping in the springs, lesson 3);
  - "frequency-dependent losses are critical for obtaining the correct decay time as a function of
    frequency."
- **Plucked and struck strings.** G. Scavone, MUMT 618 notes (McGill, caml.music.mcgill.ca):
  - "The strength of excitation of the nth vibrational mode is inversely proportional to the square
    of the mode number" for a pluck (lesson 7);
  - "A string plucked at 1/nth the distance from one end will not have energy at the nth partial and
    its integer multiples" (lessons 4 and 5, where n = 2 at the centre).
- **Drums.**
  - K. Forinash and W. Christian, *Sound: An Interactive eBook*, 12.1.3 "Drums" (LibreTexts). An ideal
    round membrane's first overtone is 1.59 times the fundamental.
  - The other ratios quoted (2.14, 2.30) are computed in `h1_claims.mjs` from the zeros of the Bessel
    functions J0, J1 and J2, which set an ideal membrane's mode frequencies.
- **Engine behaviour relied on.**
  - Stage 1 note (`resonator-stage1-…`): the engine tunes the loudest partial at the strike and mic
    the body was built with.
  - Stage F note: the plate's measured T60.

## 4. Natural-behaviour evidence
Crouch et al. measured real students in a real course (not a scripted example). That is the one
human-behaviour finding this design relies on. How Alex actually uses the tab can only be observed on
his phone (section 10).

## 5. Mechanism
- **Lessons.** Each lesson sets a known base instrument from a fresh template. It applies one change
  (A or B) and plays E4 at strength 0.8, held for the lesson's hold time.
  - E4 rather than E3: an iPhone speaker reproduces 330 Hz far better than 165 Hz.
  - The strength is fixed. The pads' random strength (0.55–1) would add a second difference.
- **Presets and lessons are built from templates.** Each body is built with the shape's own strike
  and mic (the tuning reference), then the strike and mic are moved. That is exactly what picking a
  shape and then moving them does, so a lesson or preset never tunes a different partial than the
  user would get by hand.
- **The tuning reference in saved presets.**
  - Every new body now records the strike and mic it was built with (`postStruct`); a snapshot saves
    them (`ref`).
  - `restore()` builds with that reference, then moves the strike and both mics to where they were
    saved.
  - A preset from before this stage has no reference and is built as before.

## 6–7. Competing explanations, corrections, limits
- **Loudness can masquerade as brightness.**
  - A fully soft mallet (1.0) at E4 measured 35 dB quieter than a hard one: "darker" would have been
    heard as "almost silent".
  - The lesson uses 0.6, measured 7 dB quieter, and says so in its explanation.
- **Pitch also moves brightness** (J. Marozeau and A. de Cheveigné, "The effect of fundamental
  frequency on the brightness dimension of timbre", *JASA* 121(1), 383–387, 2007, read 2026-10-12).
  Every lesson keeps the key fixed, so no comparison crosses pitches.
- **First drafts failed their own checks:**
  - "the 2nd and 4th partials fall more than 70 dB" (the 4th was already inaudible: −69 → −106 dB);
  - "hollow, like a clarinet" (with a firm mallet the odd partials were also weak);
  - "about 3 dB / 20 dB" for strike vs pluck (measured 5 to 16 dB);
  - an envelope "on tone" in oscillator mode (a sine drive has no partials to open).

  Each lesson's text now quotes what was measured.
- **The preset "Feedback organ" was dropped.** Its self-oscillation landed about 160 to 570 cents from the
  key on the Hoop (the String and Tine were further off or fell silent), and the output sat at full scale
  whatever the Level (the output's tanh saturates). "Turning bell" replaced it.
- **The "Breathing pad" changed exciter.** In oscillator mode the pad's draft settings rose 11–12 dB
  from E3 to E5, so the pad uses long mallet strings with a tone envelope. Measured flat across keys
  within 1.1 dB.
- **Not fixed here (engine or wider page changes; for Alex to decide as their own stages):**
  - The octave trap can still occur within a session. After moving the strike and mic near an end,
    changing Size or editing the body rebuilds with the moved pickups and may tune the 2nd partial.
    Keeping the build-time reference sticky would fix it.
  - Oscillator mode gets louder up the keyboard. From E3 to E5 (single keys, LUFS): 8 dB on the
    default string, 9 dB on the tine, 11 dB on the chime, 11–12 dB with the pad's draft settings.
    (Corrected 2026-10-12: an earlier version of this line, and the message to Alex, gave 11–12 dB
    for all of them.)
  - Feedback past half way saturates the output.
  - The heavy-body note can flash with the previous body's oversampling.
- **Limits.**
  - Crouch et al. studied a university physics course. That predicting helps people learning sound on
    a phone is a reasonable transfer, not a tested one.
  - The material words in lesson 3 ("metal", "wood") are an analogy for the measured behaviour, not a
    measured claim.

## 8. Context transfer
- The lessons quote this engine's own numbers (measured at 22.05, 32 and 48 kHz), not textbook values;
  the textbook ratios are quoted beside them as such.
- Only the phone can show whether the differences are audible through its speaker. The lessons play E4
  for that reason.

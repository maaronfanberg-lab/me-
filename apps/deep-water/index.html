What you're hearing
  • Every sound is an air bubble deep under the sea, heard from underwater. Its pitch is the Minnaert resonance: f = (1/2πa)·√(3γP/ρ). Bigger bubbles are lower; deeper water (more pressure) raises the pitch.
  • As a bubble rises it swells, so its note slowly sinks, and its wake keeps it murmuring. Big ones rise at 0.711·√(g·2a) (Davies & Taylor).
  • Wikipedia edits from all languages, live: the size of the change sets the bubble size (a 10-byte fix is a 2 cm bubble, a big new section is 20 cm). New pages release a chain of bubbles, bots a small cluster. Each language wiki has its own place and depth in the water.
  • Earthquakes worldwide (US Geological Survey, checked every minute): each new quake releases a giant bubble far below, its size from the magnitude, its depth from the quake's depth, left or right from where on Earth it was.
  • The deep hum swells a little when the world is busier.
  • If the data can't be reached, Alex's Morel attractor releases the bubbles instead.
  • No surface, no splashes, no running water: only the deep.

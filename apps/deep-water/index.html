What you're hearing
  • Direct waveform audification: the measured vertical-component waveform is sent to both audio channels. There are no tones, bubbles, oscillators, random generators, or reverberation in this mode. Every wiggle in the signal produces a corresponding wiggle in the audio output.
  • Necessary transformations: the trace is centered and uniformly peak-scaled, replayed faster to move ground vibrations into audible frequencies, and passed through a 15 Hz subsonic-protection high-pass filter. This is not a calibrated measurement of acoustic pressure.
  • Replay: the most recently downloaded recording is looped and refreshed. It is a recent recording, not a zero-latency live stream.
  • Every sound is an air bubble deep under the sea, heard from underwater. Its pitch is the Minnaert resonance: f = (1/2πa)·√(3γP/ρ). Bigger bubbles are lower; deeper water (more pressure) raises the pitch.
  • As a bubble rises it swells, so its note slowly sinks, and its wake keeps it murmuring. Big ones rise at 0.711·√(g·2a) (Davies & Taylor).
  • Wikipedia edits from all languages, live: the size of the change sets the bubble size (a 10-byte fix is a 2 cm bubble, a big new section is 20 cm). New pages release a chain of bubbles, bots a small cluster. Each language wiki has its own place and depth in the water.
  • Without a seismometer, every bubble is one real earthquake from the last 24 hours: its size comes from that quake's magnitude (radius = 1 cm × 10^(0.2·M): M1 ≈ 1.6 cm, M5 = 10 cm), its depth in the water from the quake's depth (10 m + 0.5 m per km), left/right from its longitude, distance from its latitude. So each pitch is that quake's own numbers: many small shallow quakes ring high, rare big deep ones low. Quakes of M4 and up release a short chain. Recent and larger quakes speak more often, and none repeats until six others have spoken. A brand-new quake booms at a pitch set by its magnitude (60 Hz small, 24 Hz great).
  • Earthquakes worldwide, live from the US Geological Survey (checked every minute): every quake of the last 24 hours is a vent on the sea floor. Each new quake opens with a giant bubble, then its vent keeps releasing bubbles the way aftershocks fade (Omori's law), so a big recent quake keeps bubbling for hours while small old ones fall quiet. Bigger magnitude, bigger bubbles; deeper quake, deeper bubbles; west/east of the globe, left/right.
  • Hear the Earth move: a real seismometer's record of the ground's vertical motion, the last 20 minutes played 20 times faster as a one-minute loop that refreshes every minute (1 Hz shaking becomes a 20 Hz rumble). When an earthquake's waves reach the station you hear them, see them in the trace at the bottom, and more bubbles rise while the ground shakes.
  • The deep hum swells a little when the world is busier.
  • If the data can't be reached, Alex's Morel attractor releases the bubbles instead.
  • No surface, no splashes, no running water: only the deep.
